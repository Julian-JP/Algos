  <h2 style="color:#2b4a6f;text-align:center;">Kruskal's Algorithm for Minimum Spanning Tree</h2>

  <p>
    <strong>Kruskal's Algorithm</strong> finds a <em>minimum spanning tree (MST)</em> of a connected, undirected graph.
    An MST connects all vertices with the minimum total edge weight, without forming cycles.
    It is widely used in network design, clustering, and circuit design.
  </p>

  <h3 style="color:#2b4a6f;">Construct MST</h3>
  <p style="font-size:0.95em;margin-top:-8px;margin-bottom:10px;color:#666;">
    <strong>Time Complexity:</strong> O(E log E) &nbsp;&nbsp; | &nbsp;&nbsp; <strong>Space Complexity:</strong> O(V)
  </p>
  <p><strong>Goal:</strong> Select edges to connect all vertices with minimum total weight without forming cycles.</p>
  <ol>
    <li>Sort all edges in the graph by their weight in ascending order.</li>
    <li>Initialize a <strong>disjoint-set (union-find) structure</strong> to track connected components of vertices.</li>
    <li>Iterate over the sorted edges:
      <ul>
        <li>For each edge (u, v):
          <ul>
            <li>If u and v belong to different components (not already connected):
              <ul>
                <li>Add the edge to the MST.</li>
                <li>Union the components containing u and v.</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>If u and v are already in the same component, skip the edge to avoid cycles.</li>
          </ul>
        </li>
      </ul>
    </li>
    <li>Continue until the MST contains exactly (V - 1) edges.</li>
  </ol>

  <h3 style="color:#2b4a6f;">Notes</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Kruskal’s algorithm works for both connected and disconnected graphs, producing a <em>minimum spanning forest</em> in the latter case.</li>
    <li>Using a union-find data structure with path compression ensures nearly linear-time component tracking.</li>
    <li>The MST is guaranteed to have the minimum total weight, but it may not be unique if edges have equal weights.</li>
    <li>Edges are always added in increasing order of weight to maintain optimality.</li>
  </ul>
